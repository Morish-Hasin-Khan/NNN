# The number is not the story

Every few months a figure surfaces that looks damning on its own: money in, very
little money out. The gap gets a headline, the headline gets a hashtag, and the
argument ends there. But a balance is not a verdict. Funds are allowed to hold
money. Endowments are supposed to. The question an audit exists to answer is
narrower and harder: was the money held for a stated purpose, and does the
holding match that purpose?

# What to look for instead

Read the audit for three things. First, the mandate — what the fund said it would
do with the money when it asked for it. Second, the disbursement schedule, which
tells you whether slow spending was planned or simply happened. Third, the
auditor's own language: statutory auditors are careful people, and a qualified
opinion is a much louder signal than a large unspent balance.

# Why this matters beyond one fund

Public money attracts two failures at once — the failure to spend it well, and
the failure to scrutinise it well. A press cycle that treats every unspent rupee
as theft trains readers to ignore the cases that are theft. Precision is not
pedantry here. It is the only thing that makes the accusation mean anything when
it is finally warranted.
